# Gull species source material — 19 October 2026

Adult breeding-plumage references were used to keep the three character species distinct. These are anatomy/colour references; the original diner and updated human cast supply the game's rendering style. The photographs below are research inputs, separate from the newly generated concept artwork.

## Herring gull — Larus argentatus

- [RSPB species guide](https://www.rspb.org.uk/birds-and-wildlife/herring-gull): pale grey upperparts, white head/body, pink legs, substantial yellow bill with red lower-bill spot, and black outer wing feathers with white spots.
- [WWT gull identification guide](https://www.wwt.org.uk/discover-wetlands/wetland-wildlife/meet-the-family/gulls-and-terns-found-in-uk-wetlands): adult plumage and comparison with the black-backed gulls. Its adult breeding-plumage photograph is saved as `research/herring-gull-wwt.jpg`; the individual photographer is not identified in the retrieved page text.
- [Source photograph](https://www.wwt.org.uk/uploads/images/2024-01-23/herring-gull-adult-breeding-plumage-717x403.jpg).

Design translation: balanced medium build, pale grey folded wing, readable pink feet, stout yellow bill/red spot and pale iris. White tail remains separate from the folded black wingtip feathers.

## Black-legged kittiwake — Rissa tridactyla

- [RSPB species guide](https://web-cdn.rspb.org.uk/birds-and-wildlife/kittiwake): small yellow bill, dark eye, black legs, white underside, grey upperparts, and black wingtips without white spots.
- [BTO BirdFacts](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/kittiwake): adult plumage and species identity.
- [RSPB reference photograph](https://base-prod.rspb-prod.magnolia-platform.com/.imaging/focalpoint/_WIDTH_x_HEIGHT_/dam/jcr%3A2082546e-645f-422c-8cb8-69a56dc8238e/RSPB_1018839-Species-Kittiwake-dult%20sat%20on%20clifftop-Bempton-Ben%20Andrew.jpg), saved as `research/kittiwake-rspb.jpg`. The source filename identifies Ben Andrew.

Design translation: smaller compact gull with a fine plain yellow bill, dark eye, black feet and clean black wing ends. No red bill spot, black head hood, juvenile wing band or tern tail streamers. Its faster game role is the user's design direction, not a claim that this species is necessarily faster than both other species in nature.

## Great black-backed gull — Larus marinus

- [RSPB species guide](https://www.rspb.org.uk/birds-and-wildlife/great-black-backed-gull): very large, heavy build, powerful bill, black upperparts, white body/head, pink legs and white outer-wing edging.
- [WWT comparison](https://www.wwt.org.uk/discover-wetlands/wetland-wildlife/meet-the-family/gulls-and-terns-found-in-uk-wetlands): broad wings, very heavy yellow/red bill and dull pink legs; helps avoid confusion with the yellow-legged lesser black-backed gull.
- [RSPB reference photograph](https://base-prod.rspb-prod.magnolia-platform.com/.imaging/focalpoint/landscape16to9/_WIDTH_x_HEIGHT_/dam/jcr%3A50e70068-2a65-4d32-a265-e6a2bc06b6a4/2116182-Species-Great-Black-Backed-Gull-on-cliff-Ben%20Andrew.jpg), saved as `research/great-black-backed-gull-rspb.jpg`. The source filename identifies Ben Andrew.

Design translation: broad white chest, heavier neck/head/bill, charcoal-black upperwings and pale pink feet. Three variants explore upright, rounded dominant and broader hunched silhouettes.
