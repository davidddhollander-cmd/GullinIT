# Retro character pilot: pier diner

Created 14 October 2026 as the first sample for a proposed cast-wide 32-bit-era pixel-art overhaul. Revised 15 October with substantially larger, square pixels. The original 16 × 24 placeholder remains at `assets/archive/sprites/pier_diner.png`; the live character combines `assets/archive/sprites/pier-diner-retro-large-pixels.png` with `assets/archive/sprites/pier-diner-motion-large-pixels.png`. The earlier pilot sheets remain alongside them for comparison. Both live sheets are transparent 4 × 3 sheets, shown with nearest-neighbour filtering at about 44 logical pixels high. The artwork was made with the built-in image-generation tool, then mapped without changing its pixels by `tools/map_pier_diner_sheet.py`.

The first twelve poses cover calm variation, first suspicion, tracking, full alert, bracing, raised-hand windup, swat, follow-through and annoyed recovery. The companion sheet adds four-frame walk, run and snack-eating cycles. `PierDinerArt` plays walking on the return home, running during a chase, and eating at intervals while calm and carrying food. The fried fish remains a separate stealable sprite; it rises to the mouth during eating and moves to the holding hand in alert and swat poses. The swat art starts on an actual close-range hit. The original umbrella and scoring mechanics are unchanged.

This is a style pilot, not a final animation master: both sheets are generated and some walk frames remain similar. The surrounding cast and scenery retain their current mixed styles. Compare `test-output/retro-diner-walk.png`, `retro-diner-run.png`, `retro-diner-eat.png`, `retro-diner-alert.png`, `retro-diner-windup.png` and `retro-diner-swat.png` at 1280 × 720 before applying the look to other characters.

## Generation prompts

Larger-pixel revision (built-in image edits, 15 October): preserve all poses, costume, cell order, transparent background and feet baseline. Repaint on a roughly 48-pixel-tall virtual character grid with square output blocks about 6–10 pixels wide, stepped contours, flat colour areas and roughly 20–24 colours. Remove fine dithering, gradients and smooth illustration edges. Use the revised pose sheet as the style reference for the walk/run/eat sheet.

Initial built-in generation: one consistent seaside pier diner, dark skin, short wavy hair under a coral visor, teal-and-cream striped shirt, navy shorts and sandals. Exact four-column, three-row transparent sheet of 12 full-body poses: calm idle and eating mime; escalating suspicion and alert; bracing, swat windup, open-palm strike, follow-through and annoyed recovery. Bright high-colour 32-bit-era pixel-art cartoon style, bold outline, consistent scale and feet baseline, no food or props painted into the frames.

User revision, built-in image edit of the first sheet: keep the same identity, colours, 12 poses, layout, transparent alpha and silhouettes; repaint as much chunkier retro pixel art with square pixels, stepped contours, hard edges, a limited 24–32-colour palette, flat colour clusters, no antialiasing or smooth illustration shading. Keep expressive pixel eyes, brows and large hands. The revised image is the one integrated into the project.

Motion companion, built-in image edit using the revised sheet as the identity and style reference: exact four-column, three-row transparent layout of the same character; row one four distinct upright walk steps, row two four forward-leaning chase/run steps, row three four snack-eating hand movements from forward hold to mouth and back. Keep the same chunky pixels, limited palette, consistent scale, facing and foot baseline. Draw no food because the game layers its separate fish sprite onto the hand.
